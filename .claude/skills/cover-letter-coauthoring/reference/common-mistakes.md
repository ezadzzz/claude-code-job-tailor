# Cover Letter Common Mistakes - Quick Reference

## Auto-Rejection Mistakes (Never Do These)

### 1. Typos and Grammar Errors
**Impact**: 80% of recruiters auto-reject
**Prevention**:
- Read aloud 3x minimum
- Use spell-check
- Have someone else proofread
- Wait 24 hours and re-read with fresh eyes

### 2. Exceeding One Page
**Impact**: Shows lack of communication skills
**Fix**:
- Tighten language (remove filler words)
- Adjust margins (0.5" minimum)
- Use 10-11pt font if needed
- Cut less impactful achievements

### 3. Unprofessional Email Address
**Impact**: 76% reject for this reason
**Bad Examples**: partygirl2000@, cooldude@, babygirl@
**Good Examples**: firstname.lastname@, firstnamelastname@

### 4. Generic Salutations
**Bad**: "To Whom It May Concern", "Dear Hiring Manager", "Dear Sir/Madam"
**Good**: "Dear Sarah Johnson,", "Dear Hiring Team at [Company],"
**How to Fix**: Use LinkedIn to find actual names

## Content Mistakes (Wastes Opportunity)

### 5. Resume Duplication
**Problem**: Cover letter becomes redundant
**Solution**: Use cover letter to tell stories BEHIND resume bullets
- Resume: "Increased sales by 40%"
- Cover Letter: "When I inherited a struggling territory with declining sales, I rebuilt relationships with key accounts by implementing weekly check-ins and custom reporting. Within 6 months, sales increased 40% and I secured our largest contract to date ($2M)."

### 6. Credential Listing Without Results
**Bad**: "I have 5 years of experience in Python and AWS"
**Good**: "Using Python and AWS, I built a data pipeline that reduced processing time from 4 hours to 15 minutes, saving the team 35 hours per week"

### 7. No Personality
**Problem**: Sounds like AI wrote it or could apply to anyone
**Signs of No Personality**:
- All formal, no conversational language
- No personal stories or anecdotes
- Could be sent to any company (not specific)
- No authentic voice

**Fix**: Write like you're explaining to a friend why you want this job

### 8. Generic Company Interest
**Bad**: "I've always wanted to work at a leading tech company"
**Good**: "When I read about [Company]'s commitment to [specific initiative], it reminded me of why I got into [field]. I've been following [specific project/news] and was impressed by [specific detail]"

## Tone Mistakes (Kills Credibility)

### 9. Overly Formal/Stiff
**Bad Examples**:
- "I am writing to express my sincere interest in the aforementioned position"
- "It would be my utmost pleasure to contribute to your esteemed organization"
- "I hereby submit my application for your consideration"

**Better**: "I'm excited to apply for the [position] role at [Company]"

### 10. Too Casual
**Bad Examples**:
- "Hey there!"
- "You guys are awesome!"
- "I'd totally crush this role"
- Excessive exclamation points!!!

### 11. Desperate Tone
**Bad Examples**:
- "I really need this opportunity"
- "I'll do anything for this job"
- "Please give me a chance"
- "I'm willing to work for less"

### 12. Arrogant Tone
**Bad Examples**:
- "I'm the best candidate you'll find"
- "You'd be lucky to have me"
- "My skills are unmatched"

**Better Balance**: Confident but humble
- "I believe my experience in [X] would be valuable for [specific challenge]"
- "I'm excited about the opportunity to contribute to [specific goal]"

## Structure Mistakes (Poor Organization)

### 13. Starting with Resume Summary
**Bad Opening**: "I am a marketing professional with 8 years of experience in digital marketing, content strategy, and analytics..."

**Better Opening**: "When I saw that [Company] was launching [new initiative], I immediately thought of the content strategy I used at [Previous Company] to achieve similar goals. That campaign resulted in 200% increase in engagement and 50K new users."

### 14. Ending Without Call-to-Action
**Bad Closing**: "Thank you for your consideration. Sincerely, [Name]"

**Better Closing**: "I'd love to discuss how my experience with [specific skill] can help [Company] achieve [specific goal]. I'm available for a conversation at your convenience."

### 15. Buried Key Information
**Mistake**: Putting most impressive achievement in middle or end
**Fix**: Lead with strongest story/achievement in opening

## Research Mistakes (Shows Lack of Effort)

### 16. No Company-Specific Details
**Signs**:
- Could swap company name and still make sense
- No mention of company products/services/values
- No reference to recent news or initiatives

**Fix**: Include 2-3 specific company details that show research

### 17. Incorrect Company Information
**Fatal Mistakes**:
- Wrong company name
- Wrong product names
- Outdated information
- Confused with competitor

**Prevention**: Double-check all company references

## Formatting Mistakes (Looks Unprofessional)

### 18. Inconsistent Formatting
**Issues**:
- Mixed fonts
- Inconsistent spacing
- Irregular margins
- Misaligned text

### 19. Wall of Text
**Problem**: No paragraph breaks, hard to scan
**Fix**:
- 3-4 sentences maximum per paragraph
- White space between paragraphs
- Use short, punchy sentences

### 20. Missing Contact Information
**Required in Header**:
- Email (hyperlinked)
- Phone number
- LinkedIn (hyperlinked)
- Portfolio/GitHub if relevant

## TMI Mistakes (Oversharing)

### 21. Deeply Personal Stories
**Avoid**:
- Health struggles (unless directly relevant to role)
- Family problems
- Financial desperation
- Divorce/relationship issues
- Mental health details (unless advocacy role)

**Exception**: Only share if it directly demonstrates relevant skills and you're comfortable

### 22. Negative Comments About Previous Employers
**Never Include**:
- Complaints about old bosses
- Company dysfunction stories
- Reasons you were fired (unless asked)
- Workplace conflicts

**If asked about departure**: Frame positively
- "Seeking new challenges"
- "Company restructuring"
- "Looking for growth opportunities"

## ATS Mistakes (Gets Filtered Out)

### 23. Missing Keywords
**Problem**: Cover letter doesn't match job description keywords
**Fix**:
- Extract 10-15 key skills/terms from job posting
- Naturally incorporate 5-7 of them
- Don't keyword stuff (reads poorly)

### 24. Complex Formatting
**ATS Can't Read**:
- Text boxes
- Headers/footers (sometimes)
- Tables
- Images
- Unusual fonts

**Safe Approach**: Simple text, standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)

### 25. Submitted as Image or Scanned PDF
**Problem**: ATS can't parse text
**Solution**: Always submit as text-based PDF or .docx

## Follow-Up Mistakes

### 26. Not Following Application Instructions
**Critical**:
- If they ask for cover letter in email body, don't attach
- If they specify format (PDF vs Word), use that format
- If they ask for specific information, include it

### 27. Not Tailoring for Each Application
**Mistake**: Copy-paste same cover letter
**Fix**: Customize 50%+ of content for each company
- Opening paragraph: 100% unique
- Achievement stories: Emphasize most relevant ones
- Closing: Reference specific company goals

## Self-Review Checklist

Before submitting, ask yourself:

1. Could this cover letter apply to any other company? (Should be NO)
2. Did I include 3+ specific, quantifiable achievements?
3. Did I tell at least one story/anecdote?
4. Can someone identify my personality from reading this?
5. Is it one page or less?
6. Did I proofread 3+ times?
7. Did I address a real person's name?
8. Did I research the company and include specific details?
9. Did I avoid all resume duplication?
10. Would I want to interview me after reading this?

If ANY answer is wrong, revise before submitting.
